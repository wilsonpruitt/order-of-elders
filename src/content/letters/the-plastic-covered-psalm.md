> "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want."
>
> — Psalm 23:1

Not too long ago I read a sermon by Alyce McKenzie comparing the 23rd Psalm to a plastic covered couch. She is always good with comparisons and imagery. Her point, however, is well taken.
I can count on one hand the number of times I’ve preached this psalm outside a funeral. I may not ever have preached it outside of a funeral. What a shame! I am reminded of a story from a man in my first appointment. As a little boy he was called to lead the Lord’s prayer at a school assembly (that should tell you something of his age). He got up before the student body and froze for a moment, then as if prompted by nausea and panic, he opened his mouth and out came these words. They’re always appropriate words, but the the Lord’s prayer they are not. This psalm is theologically rich and bold. It’s so much more than just a bunch of words we spew in rote memory.
Nor are these words that should be reserved for special occasions. I struggle to think of a time when we don’t need to remember that God’s goodness and mercy pursue us with relentless passion. I can’t think of a time when I don’t need to be led by still waters and have my cup filled to overflowing. I can’t think of a time when my soul does not need restoring.
Some of you are probably too young to have had parents or grandparents who covered their couches with plastic, just to protect it. In my memory lives a mustard yellow velvety couch covered in plastic that was just awful to sit on. It was crunchy in a way couches are never supposed to be. In the winter, it was too cold, in the summer, it made my legs sweat. But, that couch lasted long after my grandmother. As one who has replaced my livingroom sofa because of cat damage, I understand the sentiment of wanting to protect expensive furniture. But a couch is meant to sat on. A couch is meant to napped on. A psalm is meant to sung and prayed and preached.
In this psalm we hear again how much God loves us, enough to be our shepherd, to lead us to green pastures, to have us sit and feast, to pursue us with love and mercy.
Beloved, I pray these words come uncovered for you this week, that you find actual rest with them and experience anew God’s great love for you.
